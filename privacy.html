Privacy

Carrel has no account, no analytics, and no server of ours for your reading to be sent to. This page says exactly what that means, and is specific about the few moments Carrel does touch the network.

Last updated 26 August 2026

The short version

We collect nothing about you. There is no sign-up, no email, no identifier, and no telemetry of any kind. Your library, your marks and your reading history live on your device, and sync only through your own iCloud account if you turn that on — where we cannot read them.

What stays on your device

Your books and imported files, your reading position, highlights, notes and notebooks, your reading history and goals, and every setting including your typography and themes.

If you enable iCloud sync, this is stored in your own iCloud private database using Apple's CloudKit. It is governed by your iCloud account and Apple's terms. Carrel has no ability to read it, and no copy of it exists anywhere else.

The apps you hold

Carrel uses Apple's Family Controls framework, with individual authorization — an adult managing their own device. It is not configured for supervising anyone else.

When you choose which apps and categories to hold, iOS gives Carrel opaque tokens, not names. Carrel cannot resolve a token to an app, and therefore never learns which apps you selected. That selection never leaves your device, and we could not read it even if it did.

Recaps and questions

Where your device supports it, recaps and comprehension questions are generated by a model running on your phone. The text of your books and your notes is not sent anywhere for this. On a device that cannot run the model, Carrel says so and offers your own marks instead.

When Carrel uses the network

Only when you ask it to, and never in the background. Each of these is a direct request from your device to a third party, carrying no identifier of you and no account of ours. Each is subject to that party's own privacy policy.

WhenWhere it goes, and what it carries
Browsing or downloading a free book gutendex.com and gutenberg.org receive your search terms and which public-domain book you chose.
Adding a book by title or ISBN openlibrary.org and the Google Books API receive that title or ISBN so Carrel can fill in the cover and details.
Looking up a word or phrase The text you selected is sent to en.wikipedia.org to fetch a summary. Apple's built-in dictionary is handled by iOS and involves no request from Carrel.
Readwise sync — only if you set it up Off unless you paste your own Readwise access token. Your highlights are then sent to your Readwise account, under Readwise's privacy policy. The token is kept in your device's Keychain and is never sent anywhere but Readwise. Removing it stops all of this.

What Carrel never does

  • No account, sign-up, login or email address.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or attribution SDKs. The only third-party code in Carrel is the Readium toolkit, which renders books and makes no network requests of its own.
  • No advertising, and no tracking of you across other apps or websites.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data. There is none to sell.
  • Carrel cannot read your library, your notes, or which apps you hold.

Children

Carrel is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect anything from anyone. Its Screen Time authorization is for a person managing their own device, not for supervising a child's.

Your rights

Because Carrel holds no data about you, there is nothing for us to export, correct or delete — and no request you could make that we would be able to act on. Deleting the app removes your library and settings from the device; turning off iCloud sync and deleting the app's iCloud data removes the synced copy from your own account.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the previous versions remain in this page's public history.
